Formulas used

Simple regression. With SSX = Σ(X − MX)², SSY = Σ(Y − MY)² and SP = Σ(X − MX)(Y − MY):

  • Slope b₁ = SP / SSX; intercept b₀ = MY − b₁·MX; prediction Ŷ = b₀ + b₁X.
  • Residual e = Y − Ŷ; SSres = Σe²; SSreg = SSY − SSres; R² = SSreg / SSY = r².
  • Residual standard error s = √(SSres / (n − 2)); standard error of the slope SE(b₁) = s / √SSX.
  • Test of H₀: β₁ = 0: t = b₁ / SE(b₁) with df = n − 2; equivalently F = t² with (1, n − 2) df. Confidence interval: b₁ ± tcrit·SE(b₁).
  • Interval for the mean of Y at x: Ŷ ± tcrit·s·√(1/n + (x − MX)²/SSX). Prediction interval for one new Y: Ŷ ± tcrit·s·√(1 + 1/n + (x − MX)²/SSX).

Multiple regression. With the predictors in a matrix X (plus a column of 1s) and outcome y:

  • Coefficients b = (XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀy, the values that minimise SSres = Σ(y − Xb)². Each coefficient is the change in Y per 1-unit increase in that predictor holding the other predictor constant.
  • s² = SSres / (n − k − 1) with k predictors; SE(bj) = s·√[(XᵀX)⁻¹]jj; tj = bj / SE(bj) with df = n − k − 1.
  • R² = 1 − SSres/SSY; adjusted R²adj = 1 − (1 − R²)(n − 1)/(n − k − 1).
  • Overall test: F = (R²/k) / ((1 − R²)/(n − k − 1)) with (k, n − k − 1) df.
  • Variance inflation factor with two predictors: VIF = 1 / (1 − r₁₂²), where r₁₂ is the correlation between X1 and X2.
  • Partial (added-variable) plot for X1: residuals of Y regressed on X2 against residuals of X1 regressed on X2. Its slope equals the X1 coefficient in the full model.

p-values come from the exact t and F distributions (regularised incomplete beta function). Everything runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

Key: symbols and terms
X, Y
The predictor and the outcome being predicted. In multiple regression the predictors are X1 and X2.
Ŷ (Y-hat)
The value of Y predicted by the fitted line or plane.
b₀, b₁
Intercept (predicted Y when X = 0) and slope (change in predicted Y per 1-unit increase in X).
Estimate
A fitted coefficient. In multiple regression it is the change in Y per 1-unit increase in that predictor, holding the other constant.
Std. error
How much a coefficient would vary from sample to sample. Smaller means a more precisely estimated coefficient.
t, p
t = estimate / its standard error. p is the probability of an estimate at least this far from 0 if the true coefficient were 0. It is not the probability that H₀ is true.
R²
The share of the variation in Y accounted for by the model.
Adjusted R²
R² penalised for the number of predictors, so an extra predictor must earn its place.
Pearson r
Strength and direction of the straight-line association in simple regression, from −1 to +1; r² = R².
s
Residual standard error: the typical size of a prediction miss, in Y units.
F, p (F test)
Tests whether the predictors together explain more variation than chance would. With one predictor, F = t².
n, df
Number of points, and residual degrees of freedom (n minus the number of estimated coefficients, including the intercept).
Residual
Actual Y minus fitted Y: the part the model misses, drawn as thin red segments or squares.
Confidence band
The plausible range for the average Y at each X.
Prediction band
The plausible range for a single new Y at each X. It is wider than the confidence band.
VIF
Variance inflation factor: how much the other predictor inflates a coefficient's variance. Above about 5 signals multicollinearity.
Alone
The slope from regressing Y on that predictor by itself, ignoring the other.
True value
The effect used to generate the simulated data, rescaled to the plot's units.
Partial plot
Y and one predictor after removing the other's influence; its slope equals that coefficient.
Confounder
A variable related to both a predictor and the outcome that can create or hide an apparent effect when it is left out of the model.
Multicollinearity
Predictors so strongly related that the model cannot separate their effects: R² can stay high while individual coefficients become unstable.
Outlier, leverage point
A point far from the pattern in Y (outlier) or far from the others in X (leverage point). Either can pull the fitted line toward itself.
α
Significance level: the false-positive rate you accept. A result is significant when p < α. Intervals use 1 − α confidence.
Data source
Simulated data come from the sliders and presets. With My own data you paste values into the boxes and every result and plot is computed from them.
Seed
The number that fixes the random generator, so the same seed reproduces the same simulated sample.
