3How the statistics relate

Formulas used

Pearson r. r = SP / √(SSX·SSY), where SP = Σ(X − MX)(Y − MY). Slope b = SP/SSX, intercept a = MY − b·MX.

  • Test of H₀: ρ = 0: t = r·√(n − 2) / √(1 − r²), df = n − 2. Effect size: r².

Spearman ρ. Rank X and Y separately (ties get the mean of their ranks), then compute Pearson r on the ranks.

  • Without ties, ρ = 1 − 6·Σd² / (n(n² − 1)), where d is the difference between a point's two ranks.
  • The same t statistic with df = n − 2 gives the usual approximate p-value (reasonable for roughly n ≥ 10).

Point-biserial r. Code the two groups 0 and 1 and compute Pearson r with the numerical scores.

  • rpb = (M₁ − M₀)/sY · √(p·q), with p and q the proportions in each group and sY the SD of all scores (divisor n).
  • t = r·√(n − 2)/√(1 − r²) equals the pooled independent-measures t, and r = t/√(t² + df).

Phi (φ). For a 2 × 2 table with cells a, b (row X = 0) and c, d (row X = 1):

  • φ = (ad − bc) / √[(a+b)(c+d)(a+c)(b+d)], which is Pearson r on the 0/1 codes.
  • χ² = n·φ² with df = 1; p = erfc(√(χ²/2)).
  • Largest possible |φ| given the margins: √[plo(1 − phi) / (phi(1 − plo))], where plo and phi are the smaller and larger of P(X = 1) and P(Y = 1).

Standard error of estimate. SSresidual = Σ(Y − Ŷ)² = SSY(1 − r²), and

  • SEE = √(SSresidual / (n − 2)) = sY·√(1 − r²)·√((n − 1)/(n − 2)).
  • Prediction interval for a new case at X = x: Ŷ ± tcrit·SEE·√(1 + 1/n + (x − MX)²/SSX), with df = n − 2.

Comparing the statistics (Compare all).

  • Two 0/1 variables: φ, Pearson r and Spearman ρ are the same number, because ranks of a two-valued variable are a linear rescaling of its codes. φ is tested with χ² = nφ² (df = 1); r and ρ with t (df = n − 2), so the p-values differ slightly.
  • One 0/1 variable and a numerical score: Pearson r is the point-biserial r, and its p-value equals that of the pooled t test. Spearman ρ correlates the ranks of the scores with the code.
  • Two numerical variables: r and ρ use the same t formula on raw scores and on ranks respectively.

p-values come from the exact t distribution (regularised incomplete beta function) and, for φ, the χ² distribution with 1 df. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

Key: symbols and terms
r
Pearson correlation: the strength and direction of the straight-line relationship, from −1 (perfect negative) through 0 (none) to +1 (perfect positive).
r²
The share of variation in Y that is linearly associated with X (r multiplied by itself).
ρ, rs
Spearman correlation: Pearson r computed on ranks. It measures how consistently Y moves in one direction as X increases, straight line or not (a monotonic relationship).
rpb
Point-biserial correlation: Pearson r between a numerical score and a two-valued (0/1) variable such as group membership.
φ
Phi coefficient: Pearson r between two 0/1 variables, i.e. the strength of association in a 2 × 2 table.
χ²
Chi-square statistic. For a 2 × 2 table, χ² = n·φ², evaluated with df = 1.
SEE
Standard error of estimate: the typical size of the vertical miss between actual Y values and the regression line, in Y units. Smaller SEE means better predictions.
Residual
The vertical gap between a point and the regression line (Y − Ŷ), shown as thin red segments.
Ŷ
The value of Y predicted from X by the regression line.
Prediction interval
The range likely to contain the Y of a single new case at a given X. It is wider than ±1 SEE and widens away from the mean of X.
Slope, intercept
How much Y is predicted to change per 1-unit increase in X, and the predicted Y when X is 0.
Rank
A score's position when the scores are ordered from lowest (1) to highest (n). Tied scores share the average rank.
Monotonic
A relationship that goes in one direction only (always rising or always falling) but need not be a straight line.
Coding (0/1)
Labelling the two categories of a variable 0 and 1 so that it can be correlated. Swapping the codes flips the sign of rpb and φ but not their size.
Margins
The row and column totals of a 2 × 2 table. Unequal margins limit how large φ can be.
n, df
Number of observations, and degrees of freedom (n − 2 for r, ρ, rpb and SEE; 1 for the χ² test of φ).
t, p
Test statistic for H₀: the population correlation is 0, and the probability of a result at least this extreme if H₀ were true. It is not the probability that H₀ is true.
α
Significance level: the false-positive rate you accept. The result is significant when p < α.
Outlier
A point far from the overall pattern. One can shift r substantially, especially in small samples, while ranks (Spearman) are far less affected.
Leverage point
A point with an extreme X value that can pull the line toward itself (see Anscombe IV).
Dichotomy
A variable with only two values. A true dichotomy (treatment versus control) is two categories by nature; an artificial one is made by splitting a numerical variable (high versus low), which loses information and weakens correlations.
Data source
Simulated data come from the sliders and presets. With My own data you paste values into the boxes and every result and plot is computed from them.
Seed
The number that fixes the random generator, so the same seed reproduces the same simulated sample.
