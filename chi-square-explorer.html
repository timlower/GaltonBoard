Formulas used

Chi-square statistic. χ² = Σ (O − E)² / E, summed over every category or cell. p-values come from the χ² distribution (regularised incomplete gamma function).

  • Goodness of fit: E = n · weight / Σweights, df = k − 1.
  • Independence: E = (row total × column total) / n, df = (r − 1)(c − 1).
  • Yates correction (2 × 2 only): each |O − E| is reduced by 0.5 before squaring. Phi and V are always computed from the uncorrected χ².
  • Standardised residual: (O − E) / √E.

Phi (φ). For a 2 × 2 table with cells a, b (row 1) and c, d (row 2):

  • φ = (ad − bc) / √[(a+b)(c+d)(a+c)(b+d)], the Pearson r between the two 0/1 variables. Its sign depends on how the rows and columns are ordered; its size does not.
  • φ² = χ² / n, so χ² = n·φ². Effect size: φ² is the proportion of variance shared.
  • Largest possible |φ| given the margins: φmax = √[plo(1 − phi) / (phi(1 − plo))], where plo and phi are the smaller and larger of the two "second row" and "second column" proportions.

Cramér's V. V = √[χ² / (n · min(r − 1, c − 1))], which runs from 0 (no association) to 1 (perfect association). For a 2 × 2 table V = |φ|.

  • V2 = χ² / (n · df*), with df* = min(r − 1, c − 1).
  • Guidelines (Cohen): small ≈ .10/√df*, medium ≈ .30/√df*, large ≈ .50/√df*. For df* = 1 these are .10, .30 and .50.

Cohen's w (goodness of fit, and the general effect size): w = √(χ² / n). Small ≈ .10, medium ≈ .30, large ≈ .50. For a contingency table w = V·√df*, and for a 2 × 2 table w = |φ|.

Why both a test and an effect size? χ² grows in proportion to n, so with enough data a trivial association is "significant". φ and V do not depend on n, so they describe how strong the association is. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

Key: symbols and terms
χ²
Chi-square statistic: the total gap between observed and expected counts. Bigger means a worse fit to H₀.
Σ
Sum over every category (or table cell).
O
Observed count: what the data actually show.
E
Expected count: what H₀ predicts.
(O − E)²/E
One category's contribution to χ². Squaring removes the sign; dividing by E scales the gap to the size of the count.
Residual
(O − E)/√E, the signed, standardised gap. Beyond about ±2 is notable. Orange = more than expected, blue = fewer.
n
Total number of observations.
H₀ / H₁
Null hypothesis (nothing going on) and the alternative.
df
Degrees of freedom: sets the shape of the χ² curve. k − 1 for goodness of fit, (r − 1)(c − 1) for independence.
p-value
Chance of a χ² at least this large if H₀ were true. It is the shaded area beyond your statistic, not the probability that H₀ is true.
α
Significance level: the cutoff for p, and the size of the red rejection region.
Critical value
The χ² value where the rejection region begins. Reject H₀ when your χ² is beyond it (equivalently, p < α).
H₀ weight
Relative size of each category under H₀ in a goodness-of-fit test.
Yates correction
2 × 2 only: subtracts 0.5 from each |O − E| to offset the approximation's tendency to overstate significance.
φ
Phi coefficient: the strength of association in a 2 × 2 table, equal to the Pearson r between two 0/1 variables. Ranges from −1 to +1.
φ²
Phi squared: proportion of variance in one 0/1 variable shared with the other, and χ²/n.
φmax
The largest |φ| the table's margins allow. Unequal margins make a perfect φ = 1 impossible.
V
Cramér's V: strength of association for a table of any size, from 0 (none) to 1 (perfect). It rescales χ² by n and by df*, so it is comparable across sample sizes and table shapes.
df*
min(r − 1, c − 1): the smaller table dimension minus one. Larger tables need smaller V values to count as small, medium or large.
w
Cohen's w, √(χ²/n): effect size for goodness of fit (and a general effect size for any χ²).
True departure / association
How far the population being sampled is from H₀ (0 = H₀ exactly true). The population's own w, φ or V is shown under the sliders.
Margins
The row and column totals of a table.
Effect size
How large an effect is, independent of whether it is statistically significant.
Simulation
1,000 random samples drawn assuming H₀ is true. Their χ² histogram should hug the curve; the share beyond your χ² approximates p.
Single-file, dependency-free demo. Random samples use a seeded generator. Chi-square tests use counts (not percentages), need independent observations, and work best when every expected count is at least 5. A significant result shows a pattern, not its cause.
