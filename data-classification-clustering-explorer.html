Data Classification & Clustering Explorer

See how eleven methods carve up data. Break one-dimensional values into classes, find groups in two-dimensional scatter plots, and rotate multivariate data into new axes. Use simulated data or paste your own, change the settings, and copy a one-line summary of the results.

Formulas used

Classification (1-D). With sorted values x₁ ≤ … ≤ xₙ, mean μ and standard deviation σ:

  • Equal Interval: width w = (max − min) / k; breaks at min + j·w.
  • Quantile: the j-th break is the j/k quantile, so each class holds about n/k values.
  • Standard Deviation: breaks at μ + j·h·σ for step size h; with the mean as a class centre the breaks sit at μ ± (j + ½)·h·σ.
  • Head/Tail: split at the mean; the head is the values above it. Repeat on the head while head share ≤ threshold (default 40%).
  • Fit: SDAM = Σ(xᵢ − μ)²; SDCM = Σc Σi∈c(xᵢ − μc)²; GVF = 1 − SDCM / SDAM.
  • Jenks (Fisher–Jenks) minimises SDCM by dynamic programming: D(j, m) = mini [ D(i, m − 1) + SSD(i + 1 … j) ]. Natural Breaks (Jenks–Caspall) instead moves single observations between neighbouring classes while SDCM keeps falling.

Clustering (2-D).

  • Linkage distance between clusters A and B: single min d(a, b); complete max d(a, b); average mean d(a, b); Ward Δ = nAnB/(nA + nB) · ‖cA − cB‖² (the increase in within-cluster variance).
  • Gaussian mixture: p(x) = Σk πk N(x | μk, Σk).
  • E-step: rik = πk N(xᵢ | θk) / Σj πj N(xᵢ | θj).
  • M-step: nk = Σi rik, πk = nk/n, μk = Σ rik xᵢ / nk, Σk = Σ rik(xᵢ − μk)(xᵢ − μk)ᵀ / nk.
  • Model choice: BIC = 2·ln L − p·ln n (higher is better, as in mclust), where p counts the free parameters.
  • Silhouette: sᵢ = (bᵢ − aᵢ) / max(aᵢ, bᵢ), with a the mean distance to the own cluster and b the mean distance to the nearest other cluster. Adjusted Rand Index compares two partitions, corrected for chance (1 = identical, about 0 = random).

Projection.

  • PCA: eigen-decompose the covariance matrix (the correlation matrix when standardizing), Σv = λv. Variance explained by component j is λj / Σλ; scores are z = (x − μ)ᵀv.
  • ICA: whiten the data, x̃ = D−½Eᵀ(x − μ), then run FastICA: w⁺ = E[x̃ g(wᵀx̃)] − E[g′(wᵀx̃)] w followed by normalisation, with contrast g = tanh, cubic or exponential.
  • Non-Gaussianity: excess kurtosis m₄ / m₂² − 3. Recovery score: mean absolute correlation between each recovered component and its best-matching true source.

Key: symbols and terms

Classification (1-D)

k
Number of classes you ask for. Standard Deviation and Head/Tail Breaks decide their own.
SDAM
Sum of squared deviations from the array mean: the total variation in the data.
SDCM
Sum of squared deviations from the class means: the variation left inside classes. Smaller is better.
GVF
Goodness of Variance Fit = 1 − SDCM / SDAM. Ranges from 0 to 1; higher means classes that are more internally alike.
μ, σ
Mean and standard deviation of the values.
Skewness
Lopsidedness. Near 0 is symmetric; above 1 is a long right tail.
Head / tail
Values above the mean are the head; the rest are the tail.
Class range
A class holds values greater than its lower bound and up to and including its upper bound (the first class includes the minimum).

Clustering (2-D)

Linkage
How the distance between two clusters is defined: single (closest pair), complete (farthest pair), average (mean of all pairs), Ward (smallest increase in within-cluster variance).
Dendrogram
Tree of merges. Height is the distance at which two clusters joined. A horizontal cut gives clusters.
Silhouette
How well each point fits its own cluster compared with the nearest other one, averaged. From −1 to 1; above 0.5 is good separation.
ARI
Adjusted Rand Index: agreement with the true groups the data were generated from. 1 is perfect; 0 is no better than chance.
πk, μk, Σk
Mixing weight, mean and covariance of component k in a Gaussian mixture.
Responsibility
Probability that a point belongs to component k, given the current parameters. Point colours blend by responsibility.
E-step / M-step
E: compute responsibilities from parameters. M: recompute weights, means and covariances from responsibilities.
Log-likelihood
How probable the data are under the model. EM never lets it decrease.
BIC
2 · log-likelihood − (parameters) · ln n. Rewards fit, penalises complexity. Higher is better (the convention used by the R package mclust).
EII, VII, EEE, VVV
Covariance structures. First letter: volume (E equal, V varying); second: shape; third: orientation. I = identity (circular). EII equal circles, VII circles of different sizes, EEE identical ellipses, VVV free ellipses.

Projection

Eigenvalue
Variance captured by a principal component.
Loading
Weight of an original variable in a component. Large magnitude means that variable matters to that component.
Score
A case’s coordinate on a component.
Scree plot
Bar chart of eigenvalues; look for the elbow where bars level off.
Standardize
Rescale each variable to unit variance first (PCA on the correlation matrix) so large-unit variables do not dominate.
Whitening
Rotating and scaling data so the variables are uncorrelated with unit variance. ICA’s first step.
Excess kurtosis
Non-Gaussianity: 0 for a Gaussian, negative for flat (uniform) data, positive for spiky data.
Recovery score
Average absolute correlation between each recovered component and the best-matching true source (1 is perfect). Sign and order are ignored because they are arbitrary.

At a glance

MethodHow it worksReach for it when…Watch out for…
Equal IntervalDivides the range (max − min) into k classes of identical width.Data are spread fairly evenly and you want a simple, easy-to-read legend.Skewed data or outliers leave some classes empty and cram most values into one or two.
QuantilePuts the same number of observations (about n/k) in every class.You care about rank, or want a visually balanced map or table.Breaks ignore gaps in the data, so near-identical values can be split apart and very different ones lumped together.
Standard DeviationSets breaks at the mean plus or minus multiples of the standard deviation.Data are roughly bell-shaped and you want to show how far values sit from average.Misleading for skewed data; the number of classes is set by the data, not by you.
Head/Tail BreaksSplits at the mean, then keeps splitting the above-mean “head” while it remains a small minority (default ≤ 40%).Heavy-tailed data with many small and few very large values (cities, wealth, followers).Pointless for symmetric data: the head is about half the data, so it stops after one split.
Natural BreaksJenks–Caspall style heuristic: start with trial breaks, then shift single observations between neighbouring classes while within-class variance keeps falling.You want breaks that follow the natural clumps and gaps in the data, found quickly.Stops at a local optimum, so the result depends on the starting breaks.
Jenks OptimizationFisher–Jenks dynamic programming: searches every way to cut the sorted data into k classes and keeps the one with the smallest within-class variance.You want the best-fitting k classes for one-dimensional data, every time.Slower for large n; k is still your choice; breaks are specific to one data set, so legends cannot be compared across maps.
Hierarchical ClusteringStarts with every point alone and repeatedly merges the two closest clusters; you cut the resulting dendrogram at any height.You want structure at every scale, do not know k in advance, and n is small to moderate.Merges can never be undone; single linkage chains, complete and Ward favour compact round groups; memory grows with n².
Expectation-MaximizationFits a mixture of Gaussians by alternating soft assignment of points to components (E-step) and re-estimating each component (M-step).Groups overlap or are elliptical and you want membership probabilities, not hard labels.You must choose k; the result depends on the starting point; non-elliptical shapes (moons, rings) are split badly.
Model-Based ClusteringFits Gaussian mixtures with several covariance structures and several values of k, then picks the best by BIC.You do not know how many groups there are or what shape they have, and want a principled comparison.Assumes Gaussian-shaped groups; BIC may add extra clusters to approximate curved shapes.
Principal Component AnalysisRotates the axes to the directions of greatest variance, giving uncorrelated components ranked by variance explained.You want to compress many correlated variables or see them in two dimensions.Linear only; sensitive to units (standardize!); high variance does not always mean important.
Independent Component AnalysisFinds directions that make the components as statistically independent (as non-Gaussian) as possible, after whitening.Unmixing signals that were added together (cocktail party, EEG artifacts).Needs non-Gaussian sources and a linear mixture; order, sign and scale of components are arbitrary.
