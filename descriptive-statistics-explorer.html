Formulas used

Frequency distribution. Each row lists a score (or group of scores) X and its frequency f, with n = Σf.

  • Proportion p = f / n; percent 100·f / n; cumulative percent = running total of the percents from the lowest score up.

Central tendency.

  • Mean M = ΣX / n (sample) or μ = ΣX / N (population).
  • Median: the middle score of the sorted list (average of the two middle scores when n is even). It splits the distribution in half.
  • Mode: the most frequent score; there can be several (bimodal) or, for categories, a modal category.

Dispersion.

  • Range = max − min.
  • Sum of squares SS = Σ(X − M)².
  • Sample variance s² = SS / (n − 1); sample standard deviation s = √s². For a whole population divide by N instead: σ² = SS / N, σ = √σ².
  • Skewness g₁ = [Σ(X − M)³ / n] / [SS / n]3/2: positive when the long tail is on the right, where the mean is pulled above the median.

Position and the normal curve.

  • Quartiles Q1, Q2 (median) and Q3 by linear interpolation between sorted scores; IQR = Q3 − Q1. Common outlier fences: Q1 − 1.5·IQR and Q3 + 1.5·IQR.
  • z-score z = (X − M) / s: distance from the mean in standard deviations. Percentile rank = percent of scores below X plus half of those equal to X; the normal-curve percentile is Φ(z).
  • For a normal distribution about 68% of scores fall within ±1 SD, 95% within ±2 SD and 99.7% within ±3 SD.

Non-numerical data. Proportion f / N, percent 100·f / N, mode = category with the largest f. For ordered (ordinal) categories add the cumulative percent; the median category is the first where it reaches 50%.

Rating scales. Item mean, SD, median, mode and the percent choosing the top two options; a respondent’s total score is the sum of their item ratings, summarized with M, s and the range.

Observation measures.

  • Frequency method: the number of bouts. Duration method: total time in behavior, and its share = total time / session length.
  • Interval method (partial-interval): divide the session into K equal intervals and score each 1 if the behavior occurred at any time in it; report occurred / K.
  • Percent agreement po = (a + d) / N; chance agreement pe = Σ (row total × column total) / N²; Cohen’s κ = (po − pe) / (1 − pe).

Change in a single case. Change = Mduring − Mbefore; relative change = change / Mbefore.

Key: symbols and terms
Descriptive statistics
Statistics that organize, summarize and simplify results. Inferential statistics instead generalize from samples to populations.
Statistic, parameter
A statistic describes a sample (M, s); a parameter describes a population (μ, σ).
X, f
A score, and the frequency (number of individuals) with that score.
n, N
Number of scores in a sample, and in a population.
M, μ
Sample mean and population mean.
Mdn, Mo
Median and mode.
SS
Sum of squares: the sum of squared distances from the mean.
s², s
Sample variance and standard deviation (n − 1 in the denominator). σ², σ are the population versions.
Range
Highest score minus lowest score.
Q1, Q3, IQR
First and third quartiles (25th and 75th percentiles) and the distance between them.
g₁ (skew)
Lopsidedness: 0 symmetric, positive long right tail, negative long left tail.
z
A score expressed as standard deviations above or below the mean.
Percentile rank
Percent of scores falling below a given score.
Nominal, ordinal
Categories without order, and categories with a natural order. Described with percentages, proportions and the mode.
Proportion, percent
f / N and 100·f / N.
Likert-type item
A rating on a numbered agreement scale (for example 1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree).
Frequency, duration, interval
Three ways to quantify behavior: count the bouts, time them, or score each time interval for whether the behavior occurred.
Percent agreement, κ
How often two observers code the same way, and the same corrected for the agreement expected by chance. The course content names inter-rater reliability without specifying an index; these are common examples.
Case history, case study
An in-depth description of one individual without, or with, a treatment.
