Classification Breaks Explorer

See how six methods break one-dimensional values into classes. Change the data and the method, merge classes by hand, and watch the breaks, the class table, the map and the goodness of variance fit (GVF) respond.

Method guide

MethodHow it worksReach for it when…Watch out for…
Equal IntervalDivides the range (max − min) into k classes of identical width.Data are spread fairly evenly and you want a simple, easy-to-read legend.Skewed data or outliers leave some classes empty and cram most values into one or two.
QuantilePuts the same number of observations (about n/k) in every class.You care about rank, or want a visually balanced map or table.Breaks ignore gaps in the data, so near-identical values can be split apart and very different ones lumped together.
Standard DeviationSets breaks at the mean plus or minus multiples of the standard deviation.Data are roughly bell-shaped and you want to show how far values sit from average.Misleading for skewed data; the number of classes is set by the data, not by you.
Head/Tail BreaksSplits at the mean, then keeps splitting the above-mean “head” while it remains a small minority (default ≤ 40%).Heavy-tailed data with many small and few very large values (cities, wealth, followers).Pointless for symmetric data: the head is about half the data, so it stops after one split.
Natural BreaksJenks–Caspall style heuristic: start with trial breaks, then shift single observations between neighbouring classes while within-class variance keeps falling.You want breaks that follow the natural clumps and gaps in the data, found quickly.Stops at a local optimum, so the result depends on the starting breaks.
Jenks OptimizationFisher–Jenks dynamic programming: searches every way to cut the sorted data into k classes and keeps the one with the smallest within-class variance.You want the best-fitting k classes for one-dimensional data, every time.Slower for large n; k is still your choice; breaks are specific to one data set, so legends cannot be compared across maps.

Classification (1-D)

k
Number of classes you ask for. Standard Deviation and Head/Tail Breaks decide their own.
SDAM
Sum of squared deviations from the array mean: the total variation in the data.
SDCM
Sum of squared deviations from the class means: the variation left inside classes. Smaller is better.
GVF
Goodness of Variance Fit = 1 − SDCM / SDAM. Ranges from 0 to 1; higher means classes that are more internally alike.
μ, σ
Mean and standard deviation of the values.
Skewness
Lopsidedness. Near 0 is symmetric; above 1 is a long right tail.
Head / tail
Values above the mean are the head; the rest are the tail.
Class range
A class holds values greater than its lower bound and up to and including its upper bound (the first class includes the minimum).
