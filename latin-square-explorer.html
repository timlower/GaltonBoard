Latin Square Explorer

Generate and inspect counterbalanced condition orders for within-subject experiments.

Randomisation (seeded)

2 · The square

Each row is one counterbalanced order (given to one participant); each column is the order position. Hover a cell, or click a condition below to pin it.

    3 · Carryover (what follows what)

    Each cell counts how often the row condition is immediately followed by the column condition. In a carryover-balanced design every off-diagonal cell is equal. Hover a cell to see where that transition occurs in the square.

    4 · Participant schedule

    Participants are assigned to rows in order (participant 1 → row 1, …), cycling back to row 1 after the last row.

    How to choose a design
    • Order (position) effects – practice, fatigue, boredom. Any Latin square controls these: each condition appears in every position equally often.
    • Carryover (sequence) effects – one condition influencing the next. Only a balanced square controls first-order carryover: every condition immediately follows every other condition equally often.
    • Even number of conditions – a Williams design needs just n rows.
    • Odd number of conditions – a single square can’t be balanced, so the design uses 2n rows (the square plus its mirror image).
    • Number of participants – use a multiple of the number of rows so the design stays balanced. Extra participants beyond a full cycle break the balance.
    • Many conditions – complete counterbalancing needs n! rows (720 for six conditions), so it is limited to six conditions here.
    • Reproducibility – the same settings and seed always give the same design, and the page address updates so you can share or bookmark a setup.
