Cluster Analysis Explorer

See how three methods find groups in two-dimensional data. Cut a dendrogram, step through the E- and M-steps of a Gaussian mixture, or let BIC choose the model, on data that range from tidy blobs to moons and rings.

Method guide

MethodHow it worksReach for it when…Watch out for…
Hierarchical ClusteringStarts with every point alone and repeatedly merges the two closest clusters; you cut the resulting dendrogram at any height.You want structure at every scale, do not know k in advance, and n is small to moderate.Merges can never be undone; single linkage chains, complete and Ward favour compact round groups; memory grows with n².
Expectation-MaximizationFits a mixture of Gaussians by alternating soft assignment of points to components (E-step) and re-estimating each component (M-step).Groups overlap or are elliptical and you want membership probabilities, not hard labels.You must choose k; the result depends on the starting point; non-elliptical shapes (moons, rings) are split badly.
Model-Based ClusteringFits Gaussian mixtures with several covariance structures and several values of k, then picks the best by BIC.You do not know how many groups there are or what shape they have, and want a principled comparison.Assumes Gaussian-shaped groups; BIC may add extra clusters to approximate curved shapes.

Clustering (2-D)

Linkage
How the distance between two clusters is defined: single (closest pair), complete (farthest pair), average (mean of all pairs), Ward (smallest increase in within-cluster variance).
Dendrogram
Tree of merges. Height is the distance at which two clusters joined. A horizontal cut gives clusters.
Silhouette
How well each point fits its own cluster compared with the nearest other one, averaged. From −1 to 1; above 0.5 is good separation.
ARI
Adjusted Rand Index: agreement with the true groups the data were generated from. 1 is perfect; 0 is no better than chance.
πk, μk, Σk
Mixing weight, mean and covariance of component k in a Gaussian mixture.
Responsibility
Probability that a point belongs to component k, given the current parameters. Point colours blend by responsibility.
E-step / M-step
E: compute responsibilities from parameters. M: recompute weights, means and covariances from responsibilities.
Log-likelihood
How probable the data are under the model. EM never lets it decrease.
BIC
2 · log-likelihood − (parameters) · ln n. Rewards fit, penalises complexity. Higher is better (the convention used by the R package mclust).
EII, VII, EEE, VVV
Covariance structures. First letter: volume (E equal, V varying); second: shape; third: orientation. I = identity (circular). EII equal circles, VII circles of different sizes, EEE identical ellipses, VVV free ellipses.
