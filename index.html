What’s happening? A ball’s final bin is the number of times it bounced right, so it follows a binomial distribution with n = rows and p = chance of bouncing right. The red dots show the expected counts. As n grows and more balls drop, the histogram approaches a normal (bell) curve — a picture of the Central Limit Theorem. Changing the rows or the bounce probability resets the board.
