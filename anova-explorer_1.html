Testing

H₀
Null hypothesis: all population means are equal (no effect).
α
Significance level, the cut-off for p. If p < α, H₀ is rejected.
p
Probability of an F at least this large if H₀ were true.
F
Ratio MSeffect / MSerror. Close to 1 when there is no effect; larger means stronger evidence.
df, df₁, df₂
Degrees of freedom: df₁ for the effect (numerator), df₂ for the error (denominator).
F crit
Smallest F that would be significant at the chosen α.
t
Test statistic for one pairwise comparison (mean difference ÷ its standard error).
Bonferroni
Adjusted p = p × number of comparisons (capped at 1), controlling false positives.

Sums of squares & variance

SS
Sum of squares: total squared deviation from a mean. Subscripts name the source (between, within, error, total…).
MS
Mean square = SS ÷ df, an estimate of variance. MSE (or MSwithin) is the error variance.
Σ
“Sum over”: add up the terms that follow.
σ
Population standard deviation (used by the simulator).
N
Total number of scores.
n
Scores per group, or number of participants.
k
Number of groups or conditions (one-way).
a, b
Number of levels of factors A and B (two-way); a·b cells.
M, Mⱼ
Grand mean, and mean of group or condition j.
CI
95% confidence interval around a mean (the whiskers in the plots).

Effect sizes

η²
Eta squared: share of total variance explained by the effect (SSeffect ÷ SStotal). Rough benchmarks: .01 small, .06 medium, .14 large.
partial η²
SSeffect ÷ (SSeffect + SSits error): variance explained after removing other effects.
generalized η²
Like partial η² but with subject variance kept in the denominator, so it is comparable across designs.
ω²
Omega squared: a less biased estimate of variance explained (partial ω² for two-way).
Cohen’s f
Effect size in standard-deviation units. Benchmarks: .10 small, .25 medium, .40 large.
d
Cohen’s d: mean difference in pooled-SD units.
dz
Mean of paired differences ÷ SD of those differences (repeated measures).

Repeated measures

Subjects, S
Variation between participants’ overall levels. It is removed from the error term.
Cond × Subject
The error term: how much each person’s condition effect differs from the average effect. In two-way, each effect has its own (A × Subjects, etc.).
Sphericity
Assumption that the differences between every pair of conditions have equal variance.
ε (epsilon)
How far sphericity is violated: 1 = perfect, lower = worse. Multiplying both df by ε corrects the test.
GG, HF
Greenhouse–Geisser and Huynh–Feldt: two ways of estimating ε (GG is more conservative).
Mauchly’s W, χ²
Test of sphericity. W = 1 is perfect; a small p suggests a violation.

Two-way designs & assumptions

Main effect
Effect of one factor averaged over the levels of the other.
A × B
Interaction: the effect of one factor changes across levels of the other (non-parallel lines).
Cell
One combination of factor levels (for example A1 · B2).
Marginal mean
Mean of one factor’s level averaged across the other factor.
Type III SS
Each effect is tested after adjusting for all others. Equals the classic result when cell sizes are equal.
Brown–Forsythe / Levene
Test that group (or cell) variances are equal. A small p suggests they differ.
Welch’s ANOVA
One-way F test that does not assume equal variances.
Seed
Number that fixes the random sample, so the same seed reproduces the same data.

Everything runs locally in your browser; no data is uploaded. Tests use the right tail of the F distribution. Two-way independent designs use Type III sums of squares (equal to the classic result when cells are balanced). Pairwise tests use Bonferroni adjustment; Tukey HSD, simple-effects tests and mixed (between × within) designs are not included.
